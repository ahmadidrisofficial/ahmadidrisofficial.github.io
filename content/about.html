About

A builder who teaches, a teacher who builds

My full name is Ahmad Aminu Idris. Most people know me as Ahmad A. Idris. This is the story of how a childhood in Northern Nigeria turned into a career connecting education, technology and social innovation.

Where I come from

I was born and raised in Northern Nigeria. It is a place I love, and a place where the gap between talent and opportunity is impossible to ignore. The region carries one of the world's largest populations of out-of-school children, and I grew up seeing what that means in practice, including the many children in the Almajiri system living without the schooling most of us take for granted.

I do not tell this story for sympathy. I tell it because it explains my work. When you have watched children with obvious ability grow up without a route into learning, you stop treating education as an abstract policy area. It becomes personal, and it stays personal.

Engineering foundations

School worked for me. I studied hard, did well, and earned a place to study Electronics and Communications Engineering at the University of Birmingham. Engineering gave me two lasting habits: respect for evidence, and the confidence that complex problems yield to careful design.

It also gave me a jolt. Studying alongside students from around the world, I could see how much practical, hands-on technology experience my early education had never offered. That gap, between knowing theory and being able to build, became the first problem I chose to work on.

The Steamledge years

In 2017 I co-founded Steamledge Limited in Nigeria, and until 2024 I led product, technology and growth there. We started small, with twenty students and a three-person team. By 2023 our programmes had trained more than 80,000 students and around 1,500 STEM teachers, and guided more than a thousand undergraduates.

We built things that mattered to us. Allon Fasaha, our accelerated learning platform for children out of school, won the Empowering Communities category of Microsoft's #BuildFor2030 Hackathon in 2022. TechXplorer brought live coding and technology classes into schools. My Learning Friend tackled learning gaps with curriculum-aligned digital content, and Blueberry AI Tutor explored intelligent exam preparation before the current wave of generative AI made such tools commonplace.

Alongside the products, I worked with governments and development partners on the systems around them, including Nigeria's National Digital Literacy Framework, the National Digital Skills Strategy, the Smart Katsina Digital Transformation Strategy and talent programmes with UNDP.

Moving into UK higher education

In January 2024 I moved to the UK, endorsed as Exceptional Talent under the Global Talent route. Later that year I joined Fairfield School of Business as a Lecturer in Business, became a Course Coordinator, and now serve as Academic Team Lead at the Leicester campus, working across partnerships with Bath Spa University, Birmingham Newman University and Ravensbourne University London.

Higher education lets me work on opportunity from the inside of an institution. My days involve mentoring lecturers, coordinating programmes, strengthening teaching quality and helping a large, diverse student community succeed. Many of our students are the first in their families to study at this level. Widening participation is not a theme here. It is the job.

I hold an Executive MBA, a Postgraduate Certificate in Higher Education Practice and Fellowship of Advance HE, and I am currently working towards Senior Fellowship.

Where I am heading

The longer I work on education and opportunity, the more clearly I see that good ideas rarely fail for lack of merit. They fail for lack of the right support at the right moment. That is why I am now deliberately learning the world of venture capital and early-stage investment.

I am not an investor by title, and I am careful not to pretend otherwise. I am a builder and educator studying how patient, well-placed capital can help social innovations grow without losing sight of the people they are meant to serve. Over time, I want to help direct more of that capital towards the kind of work I have spent my career doing.

What connects it all

Three beliefs run through everything on this site. Talent is evenly distributed and opportunity is not. Technology only matters when it is designed around real barriers. And institutions, from start-ups to universities to governments, are the machinery through which opportunity either flows or stalls.
