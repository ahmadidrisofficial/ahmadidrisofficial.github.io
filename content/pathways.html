Education widens opportunity only when people finish, not when they sign up

I'm Ahmad A. Idris. Pathways is my weekly letter on that idea: one question a week from a five-question test I use before I try to fix anything, tried out loud on my own work, in the UK and in Nigeria.

Join Pathways Read this week's letter

Why Pathways exists

Sign-ups are easy to celebrate and easy to fake. A form filled in, a seat booked, a course started. None of it means anything actually changed for the person who did it. What changes something is finishing: staying long enough for the thing to work.

I built education technology in Nigeria, and I now teach and lead in UK higher education. I keep meeting the same gap in both places, so I built a short test I ask before trying to fix it. Each week, one question from that test, and what it looks like tried on something real.

What subscribers get

  • One short letter a week, about 600 words, written like I am actually writing to you rather than a mailing list.
  • One question from The Finisher Test, and a way to try it yourself that week.
  • Honest numbers from a live experiment I am running on my own work: a free scholarship cohort I am watching to see whether people actually finish it.
  • A standing invitation to reply and tell me how your week went. I read every reply.
